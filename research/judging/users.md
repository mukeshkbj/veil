Source files: `brief.md` and the five idea files in `research/ideas/`. This panel judges only whether a normal person would open the product again. It does not judge API depth, track fit, or judging criteria.

## How the score works

Each panelist gives a 0-10 "would I actually use this" score. The score mixes four things: would I download it, how often I would open it, how likely I am to quit, and whether I would pay or tell a friend. The idea score is the average of the five, which equals the sum divided by five. Ties break on repeat use: the idea that brings a person back more often ranks higher.

Scores are judgments from the idea text. No panelist has tried a working product.

## Strongest real daily or weekly pull

**Winner: Receipts.** It is the product-verdict check-in: "is this serum working, and should I buy the refill?" Priya and Linda score both Receipts and its twin Trial Bench at 8. Both already spend on products and want an answer. Aisha gives 5-6, Marcus and Sofia give 2-3.

**Strongest score: Headroom (6.4).** Its pull is a purchase moment, so it fires only when someone shops. Sofia's weekly cart and Priya's monthly haul make it the most used idea on shopping days. It has no check-in loop.

**Trigger moment.** Two moments, both real:

1. **The cart.** A shopper has a serum or a shade in a retailer app and hesitates before paying.
2. **Week 3-4 at the mirror.** A person has used a product for about three weeks, the refill page is open in another tab, or the bumps are up and Aisha wonders whether to apply the active tonight.

Nobody on the panel opens any of these ideas out of habit. A prompt such as "your refill is due in 5 days" would send them back in, and every panelist except Sofia accepts one reminder like that.

## Features the panel demands

All five agree on these:

1. **Honest "I cannot tell yet".** Linda and Marcus say a verdict that always says yes is worthless. A "skip" or "no change" result earns trust.
2. **No flattering score.** No one wants a 0-100 number for their face. Show change against my own baseline and a plain word. The spec admits that `ui_score` is flattered, and the panel agrees it should not appear.
3. **Works on deep skin, and says so.** Aisha asks for a visible note about how reliable the reading is on Fitzpatrick V-VI. Linda asks the same for melasma. If the app is unsure, it must say "unsure".
4. **Photos deleted after reading.** Linda, Marcus and Aisha require it. Keep the numbers, drop the face.
5. **Under 30 seconds per use.** Sofia and Priya leave at the first long setup. Three baseline selfies is the number Priya names as the limit.
6. **Money counter.** "You skipped $68." Linda, Priya, Sofia and Aisha respond to it. It is the single reason to pay or share.
7. **A doctor-ready export** for any skin condition or prescription (Linda, Aisha).
8. **A free first use.** Four panelists would pay only after they see a correct answer once.
9. **Reminders at purchase points only.** One reminder at refill time, not daily nudges.

## What the panel finds creepy or harmful

- **Friends' faces in a group room (Unanimous).** Aisha and Linda refuse to upload or store other people's faces.
- **A retailer that holds my face.** Marcus and Linda ask who sees the scan and whether a "skip" is honest when a retailer pays for the app.
- **A better face next to my real one.** The simulated "best case" or "goal" image feels like pressure to Linda and Aisha. It needs a label that says "illustration".
- **A daily mirror audit.** Daily photos and unrequested "skin scores" can feed obsessing and picking. Cap it at one look per day (Trigger Lab) and show no magnified flaws.
- **Wrong advice at a bad moment.** Aisha: "Hold on" while skin burns is harmful. Marcus: clearing a trigger that does cause a flare is harmful. Both need wording like "in your data, this period" and a clinician line that overrides the verdict.
- **Skin-tone bias.** A tool that misreads deep skin and then labels it "no problem" is harmful. Aisha says she would delete the app on the first wrong reading.
- **Asymmetry shaming (Lopsided).** "Left 2.6x right" is an odd thing to learn about your face, and a one-sided dark spot cluster may be a lesion. Needs a "see a doctor for any new or changing spot" line.
- **Teen use of actives.** The panel agrees the product must not push potent products on minors.

## Hybrid the panel would use most

**Name: Verdict.** One app that goes from "should I buy this?" to "did it work?" to "should I buy it again?"

**Flow:**

1. **At the cart (from Headroom).** Scan once. The app reads the product claims and compares them with the concerns you actually have. It says "Buy", "Skip", or "Better aimed at X". It shows a labeled best-case picture ("illustration, not a promise"). It uses the honest raw readings and never shows a 0-100 number.
2. **Start a trial in one tap (from Receipts and Trial Bench).** If you buy, the app notes the date, the product and your noise band (the normal wobble between two photos taken together). Priya and Linda said two baseline photos are the limit.
3. **Weekly check-in, 15 seconds (from Receipts).** One photo with a lighting and angle guide. The line chart shows your noise band. The verdict is "Working", "Not working", or "Cannot tell yet". Optional split-face mode (The Other Cheek) stays behind a toggle for people who want a stronger test.
4. **Active-ingredient mode (from Week 3 and Week-6 Wall).** If the product is a retinoid or an acne treatment, the app shows the expected bumpy weeks, warns about irritation, and ends with a "see a clinician" rule that overrides everything. It tells you to hold on only when redness and dryness stay steady. This is the part Aisha asked for.
5. **Refill moment (from Receipts).** Before the refill date the app shows the verdict and the cost: "Working, keep ($32/month)" or "Cannot tell, cancel and save $312 a year". One reminder, at that moment only.
6. **Makeup branch (from Already Own It and Second Shade).** In the cart for lipstick or foundation, the app shows the shades you already own that match on your face, and for foundation it flags a shade that reads off your tone. Sofia and Aisha ask for this one. Keep it as a second mode, not the main flow.
7. **Trust rules for every step:** photos deleted after reading, an accuracy note for deep skin and melasma, a doctor export, and no data shared with retailers.

**Why the panel would use it most:** it starts at the moment people already shop (Sofia, Priya), it gives the honest "skip" that Linda and Marcus want, it keeps Aisha's treatment safe, and it ends with money saved, which is the reason to pay or share.

**What to cut from the hybrid:** group rooms, daily trigger tracking, melasma scoring, and the makeup-dose slider. Each one serves one panelist or fewer and adds friction or privacy risk.

**Open risk the panel cannot judge:** whether the scores move enough between two photos to give a verdict. If the app says "cannot tell yet" most of the time, Linda and Marcus stop trusting it.
