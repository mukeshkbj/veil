# Round 2 ideation: the brand buyer

Persona: a VP of digital at a global beauty brand. I buy AR and AI beauty tech from vendors like Perfect Corp. My test for every idea: would I ship it next quarter, and which number in my weekly trading report would move?

I read `research/brief.md`, `research/decision.md`, `research/judging/sponsor.md`, `research/judging/users.md` and `research/round2/BRIEF.md` first. I did not edit any other file.

## What I learned from round 1 that shapes this file

- The sponsor judge scored a feature low when its best outcome was "buy less". I only propose ideas where the brand sells more, or returns less, or books more appointments.
- The sponsor judge discounts "sim then analyse" loops and staged time series. Every idea below shows value from one session. Where I use a simulate-then-measure step, I use it as a guard and not as the headline.
- The sponsor judge dislikes any pitch that says the vendor's output is flattered or wrong. My hair idea adds a "feasibility layer" on top of the vendor's try-on. It does not say the try-on lies.
- The user panel found no daily habit in any round 1 idea. Their best pull was a purchase moment. I name a repeat cycle for each idea and I say honestly how weak it is.

### Idea 1: Shade Twins

**Pitch.** Find the creator who has your face, then wear her exact look and buy it in your shade.

**Moment of use.** A shopper watches a creator get ready on TikTok or Instagram. She likes the look. She cannot tell whether the creator's foundation, blush and lip would work on her skin. She taps the link in the creator's post.

**User flow in 5 steps**
1. The shopper opens a link from a creator post or a product page and takes one selfie. A lighting check blocks a bad photo before any unit is spent. The page states that the photo is deleted after the read.
2. The agent reads her skin, lip, brow, hair and eye colors, and her skin type.
3. The agent ranks the brand's creator pool by tone distance and undertone, not by follower count. It shows her three twins, each with the real look photo and the SKU list.
4. She picks a twin. The agent transfers the twin's look onto her face. It then rebuilds that look from the exact SKUs, with a shade shift worked out from the color gap between her and the twin.
5. She sees her face in the compiled look, a cart with the shades chosen for her, and the creator's attribution link.

**Endpoints and units per session**

| Step | Endpoint | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Read her | `POST /s2s/v2.0/task/skin-tone-analysis` | 20 |
| Skin type for finish choice | `POST /s2s/v2.1/task/skin-analysis` with SD concerns (skin_type, oiliness, moisture) | 9 |
| Transfer twin look | `POST /s2s/v2.0/task/mu-transfer` | 3 (assumed) |
| SKU-compiled render | `POST /s2s/v2.0/task/makeup-vto` x3 | 3 |
| Correction renders | `POST /s2s/v2.0/task/makeup-vto` x2 | 2 |
| Total | | about 37 |

One-time pool build: tone analysis on each of 8 creators, 160 units. Budget: 160 pool + 30 probe + 8 live runs at 37 = about 490 units.

**Agentic loop**
- Perceive: reject the selfie if the light is uneven. Read the tone vector.
- Decide: rank twins. Compute the Lab shift between her and the chosen twin. Snap the foundation to the nearest shade on the brand's shade ladder. Pick a finish from her oiliness and moisture.
- Act: run mu-transfer, then makeup-vto with the compiled SKU values.
- Verify: measure the color gap between the mu-transfer render and the SKU render on lips, cheeks and lids, using landmark regions. If a region misses by more than a set threshold, change that SKU's value and re-render. Stop at 3 rounds or 8 units. Show the gap counter falling.
- The model decides which twin fits her occasion and writes the brief in plain words. The code decides every number.

**Signature visual.** Her face in the center. Two creator faces on each side, joined to hers by thin ribbons that carry the color gap (for example "gap 2.1"). On tap, the twin's look wipes across her face in a split. A screenshot shows three faces with matching undertone chips and a cart under them.

**Brand-client business case.** The buyer is a brand that sells through creators or TikTok Shop. The tool sits on creator link pages and product pages. Targets: add-to-cart rate for complexion SKUs from creator links, shade-related returns, and look-level AOV (one look is 3 to 5 SKUs). The pain: 56% of women with darker skin have trouble finding a shade (evidence 4), foundation returns may run near 23% (evidence 3, unverified), and TikTok Shop beauty is a $2.5B to $4.4B channel (evidence 5). Creators already carry trust (evidence 6). I would pilot it with 10 creators next quarter.

**Scores (harsh)**
- Technological Implementation 8. Four endpoints, a real verify loop and a deterministic shade ladder, but the twin ranking is a nearest-neighbor search and a judge may call it simple.
- Design 8. One selfie, three twins, one cart, and a screenshot that explains itself. The creator pool is staged for the demo.
- Potential Impact 7. The audience and the channel are large, and a client can measure it. Repeat use is weekly only if creators post weekly, and I cannot prove a lift.
- Quality of Idea 7. Using a lookalike creator as social proof, with the look transferred to the user, is fresh. Shade finders and "shade match" tools are everywhere, so the bar is "why is this not another finder".

**Biggest risk and the under-30-unit test.** The mu-transfer render may wash out or shift a deep skin tone when it copies a look from a lighter creator. The user panel named this exact failure. Test: one tone analysis (20 units) on a deep-skin face, then two mu-transfer runs (about 6 units) from a light-skin and a deep-skin reference, then two makeup-vto renders (2 units). Total about 28 units. Pass if skin tone stays within a small gap of the input and the lip and cheek color arrive intact. Fail means I drop mu-transfer and use only SKU renders from makeup-vto.

### Idea 2: Consult Canvas

**Pitch.** A consult-room tablet that turns "what do you recommend" into three priced plans a patient can see on her own face, and a clinician can sign.

**Moment of use.** The patient sits in the consult chair. The nurse has explained options. The patient goes quiet when she hears the total and says she will think about it. Evidence 14 says cost is the main barrier.

**User flow in 5 steps**
1. The nurse takes one standard selfie on the tablet. A lighting check gates it.
2. The agent maps her concerns on her face: wrinkles, pores, texture, redness, age spots, eye area. It reads her Fitzpatrick type to flag treatments that carry higher risk on deeper skin.
3. The agent builds three plans from the clinic's own price list: a small, a middle and a full budget. Each plan picks treatments that give the most visible gain per dollar and removes any treatment the Fitzpatrick type or downtime rules block.
4. The patient drags a budget slider. Her face changes between the tiers, with the matching concern masks lit up and a running price.
5. The clinician reviews and signs. The patient scans a QR code for a take-home plan with phases, prices and the clinic's home skincare products.

**Endpoints and units per session**

| Step | Endpoint | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Concern map and masks | `POST /s2s/v2.1/task/skin-analysis` with HD concerns | 22 |
| Safety flag | `POST /s2s/v2.0/task/fitzpatrick-scale-analyzer` (pre-process, then main) | 10 |
| Three tier renders | `POST /s2s/v2.0/task/skin-simulation` x3 | about 15 |
| Guard check on the middle tier | `POST /s2s/v2.1/task/skin-analysis` with 4 targeted concerns | 9 |
| Total | | about 56 |

**Agentic loop**
- Perceive: analysis with masks, plus Fitzpatrick type.
- Decide: choose a treatment set per budget with a knapsack over the clinic catalog. Each treatment lists the concerns it targets, a price, sessions, downtime and a Fitzpatrick restriction. Gain per concern comes from mask area times severity.
- Act: set per-concern intensities for skin-simulation from the catalog's expected effect, and render each tier.
- Verify: re-analyse the middle tier. If the targeted concerns do not move in the expected direction, the agent lowers the claimed gain and shows "illustration, modest effect" on that tier. The screen shows claimed gain, simulated gain and measured gain side by side. This step is a guard against over-selling. It is not the headline.
- A human gate: the clinician must approve before anything reaches the patient.

**Signature visual.** A face with concern masks glowing in proportion to the gain they will return, and a budget slider beneath it. The face changes as the slider moves, and the price ticks up. A screenshot shows the face, the masks and the three price tiers.

**Brand-client business case.** The buyer is a clinic group or a clinic-channel skincare brand (a pro brand sold through clinics). Targets: consult-to-treatment conversion, first-visit ticket, and take-home regimen attach rate. Vendors report 50 to 60% baseline conversion rising to above 85% with simulation, and a luxury skincare brand reports +53% AOV for scanned shoppers (evidence 13, 9). All of these are vendor claims. The budget-fit plan targets the stated barrier, which is price.

**Scores (harsh)**
- Technological Implementation 8. Three Skin AI endpoints with masks, a constrained optimizer and a guard loop. All three endpoints are in the Skin AI family only, so no VTO breadth.
- Design 7. The slider moment is clear. The product is a clinic tool, and a first-time judge may not feel the user.
- Potential Impact 7. Clear client and metric, but a patient uses it two to four times a year, not weekly. The conversion claims are all vendor numbers.
- Quality of Idea 6. Consult visualizers are an existing category that Perfect Corp already sells into (Keensight, Crisalix). The budget optimizer is the only new part.

**Biggest risk and the under-30-unit test.** Skin-simulation may not produce a visible, concern-specific change that the analysis can see, and the masks may not line up with treatable areas. Test: one SD analysis on four concerns (9 units), one simulation that improves wrinkle and texture at a middle intensity (about 5 units), then one SD re-analysis (9 units). Total about 23 units. Pass if the two targeted `raw_score` values rise and untargeted ones stay flat. Fail means I drop the guard step and present the tiers as pure illustration.

### Idea 3: Honest Hair Color

**Pitch.** A hair color try-on that shows what your hair can become today, what it takes to get your dream shade, and whether the answer is a box or a salon.

**Moment of use.** She stands in the hair color aisle, or sits on her couch at 11 pm, looking at a shade chart. The box shows a model with different hair. The try-on shows her in any shade she picks, even one her hair cannot reach in one step.

**User flow in 5 steps**
1. She scans the shelf QR code or opens the product page and takes one selfie with the hair visible.
2. The agent reads her current hair color, skin tone and hair type.
3. She picks a dream shade from the brand's range. The agent shows the standard try-on render for that shade.
4. The agent works out how many levels of lift that shade needs from her current level. It shows a second render of what her hair would actually look like after one box application, and a path: box now, box in two steps, or salon.
5. She gets a "color card": the box shade that fits, the care kit that holds the color, or a salon brief (start level, target level, tone, number of visits) with a booking link.

**Endpoints and units per session**

| Step | Endpoint | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Read hair, skin, eyes | `POST /s2s/v2.0/task/skin-tone-analysis` | 20 |
| Hair texture and type | `POST /s2s/v2.0/task/hair-type-detection` | 3 (assumed) |
| Dream render | `POST /s2s/v2.0/task/hair-color` | 3 (assumed) |
| Attainable render | `POST /s2s/v2.0/task/hair-color` | 3 (assumed) |
| Verify the attainable render | `POST /s2s/v2.0/task/skin-tone-analysis` on the render | 20, run only when needed |
| Total | | about 29, or about 49 with the check |

**Agentic loop**
- Perceive: read the current hair color from the tone analysis. Convert it to a level on the 1 to 10 scale with a stored lookup. Gate out photos with warm or dim light.
- Decide: compute the levels of lift to the target. Apply the brand's rules, held in an editable table: permanent box color lifts about 1 to 2 levels, previously colored hair does not lift, a dark start shows warm tones when lifted. The agent picks one of three paths and sets the attainable hex.
- Act: render the dream shade and the attainable shade with hair-color.
- Verify: read the hair color back from the attainable render and compare it with the predicted hex. If the gap is large, shift the render color and render once more. The verify step is the one that makes the render honest. It runs only when the predicted and rendered colors may differ.
- The model writes the plain explanation and the salon brief. The code decides levels and paths.

**Signature visual.** A triptych of the same face: "Your hair today", "Your dream shade" and "What one box gives you". A level ruler under the three frames marks the lift as two steps, with a red marker where the dream shade needs a salon. A screenshot says the idea in three seconds.

**Brand-client business case.** The buyer is a hair color brand (Garnier, Madison Reed, a pro line) or a mass retailer. Targets: box returns and refund requests, product-page conversion, cross-sell of a color-care kit (a gloss or a toner for warm tones), and salon-booking referrals for the pro line. The pain: home hair color is a category of more than $2B (evidence 16), 76% of UK women said their own coloring misses salon quality (evidence 17, old), and Garnier already runs a similar quiz in Walmart (evidence 18). Madison Reed reports +38% conversion for quiz plus try-on (evidence 10). My pitch to the VP: "stop selling a render the box cannot deliver, and sell the right path."

**Scores (harsh)**
- Technological Implementation 7. Tone, hair type and hair color work together with a real decision step, but the decision step is a rule table and the hair-type call adds little.
- Design 8. The triptych and the level ruler read in seconds, and the aisle QR gives a real place to use it.
- Potential Impact 7. A large audience, a clean client metric and a salon referral path. Use repeats every six to eight weeks at best, and I have no independent figure for bad results.
- Quality of Idea 8. A try-on that knows what is possible is a sharp idea. I must frame it as a feasibility layer for the brand's formula, not as proof the vendor's render is wrong.

**Biggest risk and the under-30-unit test.** The hair color the tone analysis returns may not be stable or accurate enough to infer a level, especially in warm room light or on dyed hair. Test: one tone analysis (20 units) on a photo where the true level is known, plus two hair-color renders (about 6 units), for about 26 units. In parallel, check the hex-to-level lookup offline, with zero units, against 10 hair photos with known levels. Pass if the level lands within one step on the live photo and on 8 of 10 offline photos. Fail means I ask the user to pick her current level from a chart and use the API only for the renders.

## Harsh critic: attacks on my own three ideas

### Attacks that hit all three

- **No idea scores 9 on every criterion.** I scored each between 6 and 8, and I think that is correct. The 9s need a proof I cannot get in 10 days: an independent lift number, or a live deployment.
- **Every brand ROI number is a vendor number.** A judge who checks the evidence will see Avon, Benefit, Madison Reed, Crisalix and Keensight, all self-reported. The impact case is credible but not proven.
- **The "agent" can read as a for-loop.** In all three, the code does the maths and the model writes sentences. A judge asks "what does the AI decide". The honest answer is "which twin fits the occasion", "which path suits the person" and "how to explain a plan". That is thin. The visible verify counter and stop rule are my defense.
- **Weak repeat use.** None of the three is a daily habit. Shade Twins repeats with each creator post, Honest Hair Color with each six to eight week cycle, Consult Canvas with each yearly consult. The user panel already said that purchase moments are the best pull available.
- **Face data and consent.** All three read a face. Idea 1 also stores creators' tone data. I need a visible deletion line on every screen.

### Attacks per idea

**Shade Twins.**
- The twin step is a nearest-neighbor search over eight staged creators. A judge may see a lookup table with faces.
- The core claim, that the same shade on a twin will work on you, is not proven by anything in the demo. The ΔE verify checks render consistency, not real-world wear.
- Creator photos need licenses. Using the team's own photos or stock photos with clear rights adds setup work.
- If mu-transfer fails on deep skin, the strongest scene in the video fails with it. This is the one test I would run first.

**Consult Canvas.**
- It is the least original. Perfect Corp sells to clinics and visualizer vendors already (evidence 13).
- It makes health-adjacent promises. Every simulated result must carry an "illustration" label and a clinician gate, and a judge may still feel uneasy.
- The catalog (treatments, prices, contraindications) is invented for the demo, so the optimizer's output is only as real as made-up data.
- It touches only the Skin AI family, so it scores lower on the "both families together" bonus.

**Honest Hair Color.**
- The lift rules come from public colorist practice, not from a validated source. A real colorist may disagree on the numbers. The table must be labeled as an editable brand setting.
- Hair color estimated from a selfie is the weakest link. Light, camera and dye history all disturb it.
- Hair is outside the Skin AI track, and the Skin AI judges may treat it as a VTO-only entry. The tone analysis step is my reason to keep it in the Skin AI conversation.
- Garnier already ships a similar quiz. The new part is the feasibility step, and that must show in the first ten seconds.
- I dropped a fourth candidate, "Dupe Court" (check that a cheaper dupe matches the prestige shade on your own face), after one check. It would likely reduce to a hex-distance calculation, which round 1 judges already penalized for the "Already Own It" idea.

### Ranking

**Why this order.** Shade Twins and Honest Hair Color tie at 30. The hackathon breaks ties on Technological Implementation first, so Shade Twins wins on paper. It also uses the most endpoints in a loop with a measurable verify step, and it fits the eCommerce VTO track and the creator channel.

**What I would actually buy as the VP.** I would pick Honest Hair Color for next quarter. It needs no creator partners, it drops into an aisle QR code, and the metric is simple: box returns and care-kit attach. Shade Twins needs creator contracts first. Consult Canvas sells to a different buyer and competes with tools that already exist.

**What to test first.** The probe for Shade Twins (mu-transfer on deep skin) decides the whole idea. If it fails, I rank Honest Hair Color first. The probe for Honest Hair Color (hair level from a selfie) decides the second. Spend about 54 units on both before choosing. Both fit in the 1,000-unit budget with room for demo runs.
