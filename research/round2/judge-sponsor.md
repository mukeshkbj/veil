# Round 2 judge: Perfect Corp product and developer-relations lead

Lens: would a beauty brand, retailer or clinic want the YouCam API after seeing this; does it combine many YouCam products in a way a client could not build alone; is the agent real; does it make YouCam look like a trustworthy, measurement-grade platform; is it pro-conversion. I ignored the builders' scores and critic sections. I judged each idea as a 2-minute video, 3 screenshots and a description, built by one person in about 10 days with 1,000 units. 9+ means a plausible first place out of about 150 entries on that criterion. I gave no 9 anywhere in the raw field, because none of these ideas has yet shown it.

Scores are written TI / Design / Impact / Idea (Technological Implementation, Design, Potential Impact, Quality of Idea).

## Scores

### 1. Neck Test (returns-economist): 8 / 8 / 7 / 7
- Technological Implementation 8: a closed loop of skin-analysis masks, makeup-vto renders and measured seam colour across two API families is real engineering, but one VTO endpoint does the visible work and the whole loop dies if the foundation render does not track the input hex.
- Design 8: one face, one seam and one falling number is understood in five seconds.
- Potential Impact 7: foundation returns are the right P&L leak for a brand client, but the 23% figure is low-trust, the category is narrow and use is a few times a year.
- Quality of Idea 7: using the neck as measured ground truth is smart, but shade matching is exactly what YouCam already ships, so a judge from Perfect Corp files it under "our shade finder with a counter".

### 2. Gift Proof (returns-economist): 7 / 7 / 7 / 8
- Technological Implementation 7: skin tone, skin analysis, makeup-vto, earring, necklace and scarf make the widest YouCam fan-out in the pile, but the verify step is the recipient's tap, not a machine check.
- Design 7: the two-phone story is clear, but it needs a second person in the video and risks looking staged.
- Potential Impact 7: it creates a consented customer profile and a new acquisition channel, which a retailer will value, though gift-return data is thin and the recipient may not finish the link.
- Quality of Idea 8: "measure her, do not show the giver her face" is a fresh answer to blind gifting and a real privacy position.

### 3. Money Map (returns-economist): 5 / 7 / 5 / 5
- Technological Implementation 5: two endpoints and arithmetic, and the simulation cannot verify efficacy, so the verify step is a sum on its own input.
- Design 7: dollars drawn on the face map read fast and screenshot well.
- Potential Impact 5: it keeps spend flat, which is brand-friendly, but the evidence for wasted skincare is soft and there is no repeat-use story.
- Quality of Idea 5: scan, score, recommend with a better chart.

### 4. Seam (inclusion): 8 / 7 / 7 / 7
- Technological Implementation 8: tone, analysis, Fitzpatrick and VTO feed a closed loop with real colorimetry and a blend solver, but it is the same loop as Neck Test.
- Design 7: the seam strip is clear, and the Lab ladder needs an explanation that a video does not have time for.
- Potential Impact 7: shoppers between shades and the deep-skin shade gap are a real client pain, and the "range gap" data is a useful brand story, but brand swatch hex is not measured Lab and there is little repeat use.
- Quality of Idea 7: "no single bottle fits, mix these two" is a good twist on a crowded shade-finder shelf.

### 5. Veil (inclusion): 6 / 7 / 7 / 8
- Technological Implementation 6: the core is colorimetry that a developer could write without YouCam, and tone-analysis is only a cross-check, so the YouCam share is smaller than it looks.
- Design 7: the split-cheek photo and the three-up render row read well, but the swipe step is friction a video cannot show.
- Potential Impact 7: the white-cast pain is well sourced and the health stake is real, but sunscreen is off the core YouCam client path and the dose of the swipe is uncontrolled.
- Quality of Idea 8: measuring what a product does to a face, and filling a gap that the literature names, is the most original use of the tone API in the pile.

### 6. Translate (inclusion): 7 / 8 / 6 / 7
- Technological Implementation 7: mu-transfer, analysis, tone and VTO with two own metrics is a respectable chain, and it uses a less common YouCam product.
- Design 8: reference, straight copy and translated side by side is a good screenshot.
- Potential Impact 6: the mature and deep-skin audiences are real, but there is no quantified return pain and use is occasional.
- Quality of Idea 7: "keep the contrast, not the hex" is a sound idea but sits close to Look VTO and mu-transfer themselves.

### 7. Shade Twins (brand-buyer): 7 / 7 / 7 / 7
- Technological Implementation 7: tone, analysis, mu-transfer and VTO with a shade ladder and a verify counter is a decent chain, but twin ranking is a nearest-neighbour search over a staged pool of eight faces.
- Design 7: three twins, one cart and ribbons carrying the colour gap explain themselves, though the staged pool will show.
- Potential Impact 7: the creator and TikTok Shop channel is a place where brands want to sell, but creator contracts and licences must exist before any deployment.
- Quality of Idea 7: a lookalike creator as social proof is fresh, and the unproven claim that a twin's shade works on you is the weak joint.

### 8. Consult Canvas (brand-buyer): 7 / 6 / 6 / 5
- Technological Implementation 7: three Skin AI endpoints, a knapsack and a guard re-analysis are solid, but it uses one family only.
- Design 6: a clinic tablet with a budget slider is hard for a video judge to feel.
- Potential Impact 6: the buyer is a clinic and Perfect Corp already sells into it, so this looks like our own product rebuilt, and the catalogue and prices are invented.
- Quality of Idea 5: consult visualizers are an existing category, and the budget optimiser is the only new part.

### 9. Honest Hair Color (brand-buyer): 5 / 7 / 6 / 7
- Technological Implementation 5: tone, hair-type and hair-color calls feed a rule table, and the hair level read from a selfie is the weakest sensor in the pile.
- Design 7: the triptych with a level ruler is readable in three seconds.
- Potential Impact 6: it is a real aisle use case, but Perfect Corp's clients (Madison Reed, Garnier) already run this and a feasibility layer reads partly as "our try-on over-promises", which the sponsor will not love.
- Quality of Idea 7: a try-on that knows what is possible is a sharp idea in a heavily built category.

### 10. Mirror Forecast (daily-habit): 6 / 7 / 6 / 6
- Technological Implementation 6: three endpoints with a mask-conditioned compositor are fine, but the weather layer is trivial and the loop is small.
- Design 7: a radar-style card with a bare/look slider is distinctive, yet close to a familiar scan-then-recommend shape.
- Potential Impact 6: a daily touchpoint is what retailers lack, but 11 to 16 units per user per day is an unsolved cost and the daily change in skin is mostly noise.
- Quality of Idea 6: "weather app for your face" is a nice frame with an unproven premise.

### 11. Cover Story (daily-habit): 8 / 8 / 7 / 7
- Technological Implementation 8: HD acne and redness masks become a per-lesion decision, masked concealer compositing and a re-analysis check span skin analysis, tone and VTO, which is the Skin AI plus VTO pairing the sponsor sells, and no customer would build it in an afternoon.
- Design 8: ringed spots with Cover, Leave and Treat chips and a bare/covered slider work on a still, and it is the most emotional moment in the pile.
- Potential Impact 7: a same-day concealer, corrector and patch basket is pure add-on revenue with a deadline, but use depends on a breakout and the verify step may not hold.
- Quality of Idea 7: deciding spot by spot, including "this one will not hide", is non-obvious, though it reads as a very good makeup feature and not a new category.

### 12. Fit Check (daily-habit): 6 / 6 / 5 / 5
- Technological Implementation 6: cloth-v4 plus tone plus a colour-distance sorter, with closet plumbing, and Skin AI is a minor passenger.
- Design 6: the swipe stack is fine, but eight garment photos of setup kills the first-time-user test.
- Potential Impact 5: returns are driven by fit, which no YouCam endpoint measures, so the returns case is indirect.
- Quality of Idea 5: outfit planners are crowded and the beauty tie is the least reliable output.

### 13. Second Mirror (contrarian): 6 / 7 / 6 / 6
- Technological Implementation 6: two endpoints, and the verify step is local landmark and Lab maths that could be done with no YouCam call.
- Design 7: the dark, split-face, one-caption screen is bold, but the product is voice and a screenshot cannot carry it.
- Potential Impact 6: the EU Accessibility Act is a real client driver (Douglas), but makeup-wearing blind users are a fraction and the payoff is compliance, not revenue.
- Quality of Idea 6: accessibility is rare in this field, but Estée Lauder shipped the concept in 2023.

### 14. Still Me (contrarian): 7 / 8 / 6 / 7
- Technological Implementation 7: gate, personal band, spend policy, VTO and tone make a real agent whose best decision is to spend nothing, though the weekly sentinel is local maths.
- Design 8: the brow-less selfie next to rebuilt brows, and the swatch strip with a noise bracket, are two strong frames.
- Potential Impact 6: the emotional case is strong, but the shade-drift premise has no published support and the brand risk of getting a sentence wrong with a cancer patient is real.
- Quality of Idea 7: pairing restoration with a measurement-grade sentinel is original, but the measured part may find nothing.

### 15. Pre-Flight (contrarian): 6 / 4 / 5 / 5
- Technological Implementation 6: rigorous gating and a planted-effect check, but vendor-on-vendor and mostly the Other Cheek engine.
- Design 4: a graded form and a one-page sheet is an enterprise screen with no emotional beat.
- Potential Impact 5: the access problem is real, but the buyer is a clinic and a brand client sees little in it.
- Quality of Idea 5: sensible photo QA for telehealth, not surprising.

### 16. Other Cheek (incumbent): 7 / 7 / 4 / 7
- Technological Implementation 7: gate, noise band, planted-effect test and spend policy are the most rigorous measurement in the pile and make YouCam look measurement-grade, but it uses one family, has never run on a live key, and its replay demo will be labelled as such.
- Design 7: the tension line is a good signature moment, but a four-week trial cannot be shown in a 2-minute video without constructed data.
- Potential Impact 4: its best outcome is "buy less" or "cancel", which is anti-conversion for the brand clients who pay for YouCam, and it needs weeks of data.
- Quality of Idea 7: the split-face control is clever, but it will sit among at least six other serum-verdict trackers.

## Top 3

1. Cover Story, merged with the Neck Test shade engine (call it "Cover Story with a proven shade").
2. Neck Test and Seam as one merged idea (a measured shade, with the blend recipe from Seam).
3. Gift Proof.

Reason for the order: Cover Story is the best video, uses Skin AI and VTO together in one flow, and sells add-on baskets at the moment of need. Neck Test with Seam has the strongest measurement story and the best "client can see the P&L" case, but it competes with our own shade finder. Gift Proof has the widest YouCam fan-out and a real acquisition channel, but it needs two people and relies on a human tap.

## What would raise Cover Story (merged) to 9+ on each criterion

**Technological Implementation (8 to 9).** Add the Neck Test measurement as the second verify. After the concealer and foundation render, the agent measures two things on its own pixels: the lesion-to-ring colour difference per spot, and the face-to-neck seam of the rendered shade, with the concealer hex chosen from the neck reference. Then spend 9 units on a re-analysis only if the local check is borderline, and show that spend decision in a visible notebook. Run the mask-format, composite-registers and hex-tracking probes on a live key before the build and put the real task IDs and a live screenshot in the submission, because a live run is the strongest proof for a sponsor judge. Use four families (skin-analysis HD, skin-tone, makeup-vto concealer and foundation, skin-simulation for an illustration labelled as such). Set `skin_smooth` to 0 in every measurement render and say why on screen. Keep a deep-skin guard: the face L* must not rise after any render.

**Design (8 to 9).** One hero frame carries the whole story: a ringed face, three chips, a bare/covered slider, and a small seam ring that falls from a visible break to a matched edge. One primary gesture, one screen, mobile first. The video opens on a real breakout morning, runs the slider once, and ends on the retailer view. Include a deep-skin example early. Offer a "hide rings" toggle so it does not feel like a flaw audit. Do not show a 0 to 100 face score.

**Potential Impact (7 to 9).** Add the retailer side as a second screen: a rescue-kit basket mapped to SKUs from a catalogue feed, with the lesion mix and the shade gaps per session (no photo) shown as retailer data, and a named metric per row (kit attach rate, shade-related returns, same-day pickup). Cite the shade pain (41% and 56% of women with darker skin) and the 23% foundation and concealer return figure with its low-trust flag. To fix the "only on breakout days" repeat problem, borrow the Mirror Forecast shell: a ten-second clear-day card that is cheap (a local check first, no API call unless something changes) and expands into Cover Story when the masks find a spot. That gives a weekly use story and a daily-touchpoint story for the retailer, with the unit cost kept near zero on quiet days.

**Quality of Idea (7 to 9).** Sharpen the frame to "the first beauty agent that tells you which spots not to cover, and proves the ones it did." The honest "Leave" and "Treat first" verdicts are the non-obvious part, and the measured shade seam turns it from a makeup feature into a trust feature. Do not use the words "flattered" or "wrong" about YouCam scores. Use `raw_score` silently and present YouCam as the instrument that makes the agent's claims checkable. Keep the "Treat" wording to "common non-prescription options" plus a clinician note for painful or scarring breakouts.

## Ideas many other entrants will likely also build

- Shade finders and foundation matchers (Neck Test, Seam, Shade Twins): the most common eCommerce VTO entry, and YouCam ships one itself.
- Scan, score, recommend skin routines (Money Map, Mirror Forecast): the stock Skin AI submission.
- Serum and product verdict trackers (Other Cheek): the sponsor has been warned that six or more of these will arrive.
- Hair colour try-on pages (Honest Hair Color): a heavy category for VTO entrants, though the feasibility layer is less common.
- Outfit and garment try-on planners (Fit Check): the default for the clothing half of the VTO track.
- Clinic simulators with a before/after slider (Consult Canvas): any entrant with skin-simulation will build this.

Least crowded: Veil, Still Me, Second Mirror, Gift Proof, and Cover Story in its lesion-decision form.

## Cautions that apply to the winning idea

- The two largest risks are whether concealer renders register on re-analysis, and whether the foundation render tracks the input hex. Both can be tested for under 30 units each; run them first and drop the corresponding verify step honestly if they fail.
- Every conversion figure from Perfect Corp case studies is vendor data. Use it as directional, and label it.
- Label every replay or constructed data set on screen. A faithful replay with a few real live runs reads better to a sponsor judge than a replay alone.
