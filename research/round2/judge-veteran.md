# Round 2 judge: the Devpost veteran

I score from the first 60 seconds of a video, three screenshots and the description. I read the 15 round 2 ideas and the incumbent, Other Cheek. I ignored the builders' own scores and critic sections. I did not run anything.

My scale is harsh. A 9 means the entry could take first place on that criterion out of about 150. No candidate gets a 9 on any criterion. The best candidates reach 8, and the reason is in each sentence.

## Scores

### 1. Other Cheek (incumbent)
- Technological Implementation 7: The planted-effect self-test and the personal noise band are real rigor, but with no live API run the judge sees only a replay.
- Design 7: The tension line is a memorable object, but a four-week trial cannot play in two minutes without time compression that looks staged.
- Potential Impact 6: Wasted serum money is a real problem, but the verdict arrives after four weeks and the demo shows labelled replay data.
- Quality of Idea 7: A control group on the same face is smart, but I expect several serum-verdict trackers in the pile and this one must show quickly why it is different.

### 2. Neck Test
- Technological Implementation 7: The render-and-measure loop is real engineering, but one endpoint does all the visible work.
- Design 8: One face with one falling seam number is readable in five seconds.
- Potential Impact 6: Foundation returns cost real money, but the 23% figure is weakly sourced and the shade table is mock data.
- Quality of Idea 6: Matching to the neck is sound, but shade finders are the most common beauty-AI project and YouCam already ships one.

### 3. Gift Proof
- Technological Implementation 6: Five endpoint families are used, but the only check is the recipient's tap, so the agent is mostly a loop over renders.
- Design 7: Two phones side by side is a clear frame, but a second actor in a short video reads as scripted.
- Potential Impact 6: Blind gifting is relatable, but there is no beauty-only return figure and the recipient must cooperate.
- Quality of Idea 8: "Measure her, do not show the giver her face" is the freshest framing in the shade-and-colour group.

### 4. Money Map
- Technological Implementation 5: Two endpoints and local arithmetic make a sound pipeline, but the verify step checks the idea's own input.
- Design 7: Dollar amounts drawn on the face make a good screenshot.
- Potential Impact 5: The waste evidence is a Hong Kong survey and a self-selected audience, so the story is soft.
- Quality of Idea 5: Under the map it is still scan, score and recommend, which is the shape every judge has seen many times.

### 5. Seam
- Technological Implementation 7: Mask-cleaned sampling and a secant loop on the render are solid, but the whole thing depends on how well the renderer follows the input colour.
- Design 7: The seam strip works, but the Lab ladder needs explaining and I will not read an explanation.
- Potential Impact 6: Deep-skin shade pain is real and specific, but foundation is bought a few times a year and there is no measured ground truth.
- Quality of Idea 6: The blend recipe is a nice twist, but it is a shade finder with a better answer.

### 6. Veil
- Technological Implementation 6: The drift correction and the dose-aware swipe logic are real, but the core is colour maths that does not need YouCam, so the API share is thin.
- Design 8: A face with one grey cheek and one natural cheek, plus a one-word verdict, is understood without any caption.
- Potential Impact 8: White cast is a documented reason people with deeper skin skip sunscreen, the paper cited in the file says no standard method exists to measure it, and the demo can use a real person and a real product.
- Quality of Idea 8: It measures what a product does to you, not what it adds, and I have not seen it in a pile of try-on entries.

### 7. Translate

- Design 7: The three-up of reference, copy and translated looks is clear, but the metric bars add reading.
- Potential Impact 5: Older and deep-skin shoppers are under-served, but nothing in the file measures a conversion or return problem for looks.
- Quality of Idea 6: Keeping contrast instead of colour is a good idea, but it sits next to any other "copy this look" entry.

### 8. Shade Twins

### 15. Still Me

- Quality of Idea 7: Pairing a noise band with restoration for treatment patients is rare, but the brow-and-wig part alone looks like a try-on wrapper.

- Design 4: A photo grade card and a one-page sheet is a form, and a form has no hook.
- Potential Impact 5: The wait and photo-quality numbers are real, but the buyer is a clinic and the consumer returns monthly at best.

## Top 3

1. **Veil**, built on the Other Cheek engine (see the merge below).
2. **Other Cheek** (the incumbent). It is built, it has the best rigor story, and the design is memorable. It loses to Veil on the 60-second hook and on the live demo.
3. **Still Me**. It has the best emotional frame. I rank it third because the central skin-drift hypothesis is unproven, and a video with a stand-in person for cancer treatment can feel risky.

I considered Cover Story for third. It has the best screenshot, but I expect many entrants to build blemish spotters.

## How Veil reaches 9+

The video I want to see: a close-up of a real person with deeper skin. They rub mineral sunscreen on the right cheek only. In a few seconds the cheek goes grey, a one-word verdict appears, and a difference map lights up the cast. That hook needs no caption. The merge is the key step.

**Merge: put Veil on the Other Cheek engine.** The Veil file uses two selfies and corrects lighting drift with eyebrow and eye colour. The Other Cheek design uses one photo with the untreated cheek as the control. Use that. One photo with one swiped cheek cancels light, sleep and camera drift by design. The noise band comes from the two same-day captures of the untreated face. The planted-effect self-test uses a `makeup-vto` foundation render in a known pale hex at a known coverage on one cheek. The detector must find it and must stay silent on a planted null. This fixes the weakest part of Veil (lighting noise larger than the signal) and gives it a rigor claim no other entry has.

**Technological Implementation (now 6, target 9):**
- Use the single-photo split design above, with the self-test, so the measurement has a stated sensitivity.
- Use more YouCam in the real decision. Use `skin-analysis` masks (`age_spot`, `redness`) to exclude marks from the cheek patch. Use `skin-tone-analysis` brow and eye colour as references. Use `fitzpatrick-scale-analyzer` to put the result in a skin-depth bucket. Close the loop: after the verdict, render a tinted alternative with `makeup-vto`, measure the cast on that render, and show the number falling.
- Record at least one full run on a live key. A replay-only entry caps this score at 7.
- Add a dose guide (a fixed line of product length) and show the cast as a range across thin, normal and thick application, so the dose weakness is shown and not hidden.

**Design (now 8, target 9):**
- Open on the face, not on an explanation. Hide the Lab ladder and the colour maths behind one tap.
- Use four verdict words only (Invisible, Faint, Visible, Ghost) and one amplified difference map.
- Keep the whole flow to three taps. The cheek swipe is the only step the user does.
- Show the deep-skin result first, with an honest note when the reading is uncertain.

**Potential Impact (now 8, target 9):**
- Show real data, not a staged table. Five to eight consented volunteers across skin depths, each with three to four real products, and the measured cast for each pair. That table is what a veteran judge remembers.
- Drop any prediction for products the person did not swipe, or label it as a prediction. A wrong prediction here costs trust.
- State the impact in one line from the cited sources in the file: 25% of respondents with skin of color name white cast as a barrier against 7% of White respondents (J Drugs Dermatol 2024, one medical centre), and no standard measurement method exists. Add the retailer case: a cast rating by skin depth on every product page. Use no medical claim about melasma or melanoma in the pitch.

**Quality of Idea (now 8, target 9):**
- Say the claim in one sentence: the first consumer-grade, same-photo white cast measurement, with a stated sensitivity and a noise band. The planted-effect test is what makes the claim credible.
- Do not call it a colour meter. Call it a control group for sunscreen.

**The risk I would test first:** the cast signal must exceed the noise band. The Veil file has a 21-unit test. Add one deep-skin face and one pale face. If mineral SPF shifts L* by only a few points and the band is larger, the idea fails and Other Cheek stays the entry.

## Ideas many other entrants will also build

- **Shade finders and shade matchers:** Neck Test, Seam, Shade Twins. This is the most common pattern in beauty AI, and YouCam already ships one.
- **Scan, score, recommend dashboards:** Money Map, Mirror Forecast, Consult Canvas, Pre-Flight.
- **Serum-verdict and routine trackers:** Other Cheek sits here. Its split-face control and planted-effect test are what set it apart, so the video must show them in the first 15 seconds.
- **Blemish spotters:** Cover Story. The per-lesion decision and verify loop are the differences.
- **Hair-colour try-on:** Honest Hair Color. The feasibility layer is the only difference.
- **Closet and outfit planners:** Fit Check.
- **Before and after sliders:** Consult Canvas, Money Map, Cover Story.
- **Look copy and look transfer:** Translate.

Least likely to be duplicated: Veil, Still Me, Gift Proof and Second Mirror.
